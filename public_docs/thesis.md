# State of Us — Project Thesis

## Problem
Surveys and polls are built for the person *asking*. You fill in a form, hit submit, and get nothing back. There is no satisfying way to answer a structured public question and immediately see what everyone else thinks. Participation is a chore, results are boring tables, and nothing invites you to return.

## The bet
Make the **output experience as important as the input**. Every answer is instantly rewarded with a visual, comparative, shareable result and a personal insight ("you matched 12% of the country; your city disagrees"). The loop is: **Question → Answer → Aggregation → Visualization → Reaction → Return.** One question is one self-contained experience page.

## Audience
Anyone curious about public opinion, mobile-first and thumb-driven. No account required — participation is anonymous, and skipping is always allowed.

## In scope
- One question = one experience page (answer + result + light exploration together)
- A spread of playful interaction modes and a rewarding primary visualization per result
- A **mandatory, template-driven** personal-insight layer on every result (never generative)
- Visible sample size and vote count (trust shown, never dominant)
- A curated, multi-source homepage; a launch bank covering every category and interaction mode

## Out of scope (deliberately)
- Engagement farming — XP, streaks, infinite feeds
- Forcing maps where geography is irrelevant; secondary charts that just repeat the primary
- Personalized share-image exports; analytics rabbit holes
- Accounts/auth at launch (migration hooks only)

## Success criteria
Completion *feels* rewarding rather than administrative; the launch question bank covers all categories and interaction modes; a visitor can answer, understand the result, and see how they compare in well under a minute.
